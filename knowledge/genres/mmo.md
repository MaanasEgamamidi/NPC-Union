# MMO (massively multiplayer online)

## Core loop

- Session loop (one evening): log in, meet the group or guild, do an activity (dungeon, raid, gathering, PvP, trade), collect rewards, log out with a goal for next time.
- Weekly loop: reset-based lockouts, weekly caps, and guild events pace progress and give players a fixed day to plan around.
- Seasonal loop: new raid tiers, patches, expansions, or territory seasons change the goal and reset the gear or power ladder.
- Underneath all three: a shared world and economy where other players are the main content and the main reason to return.

## Design pillars

- Other people are the content. Grouping, guilds, trade, and rivalry must matter more than solo progress.
- A persistent world with consequence. What players do (trade, build, destroy, claim) should outlast the session.
- A closed economy. Every faucet needs a sink; item destruction, fees, and repairs keep value stable.
- Pacing over volume. Lockouts and caps make content last and keep the population on similar steps.
- Legible roles. Players should know what they bring to a group (for example tank, healer, damage, or crafter, scout, trader).
- Sustainable cadence. The design must match what the team can ship every few months.

## Common failures

- Faucets without sinks: currency inflation makes new players poor and old players bored.
- Empty endgame for most players: top guilds clear content in days while the rest see nothing new.
- Chore lists: too many dailies and weeklies turn play into obligation and cause burnout.
- Role shortage: too few tanks and healers creates long queues for damage players.
- Gear treadmill obsoletes old zones and splits the population by tier.
- Dead leveling zones a year after launch, which hurts new players.
- Unchecked full-loot PvP with no safe tier drives off newcomers.
- Launch capacity failures: long queues and server instability at launch (Final Fantasy XIV 2010 is the classic case).
- Gather loops with tradeable output invite bots and gold sellers.
- Realm population decline with no merge or cross-realm plan.

## Scope traps

- "Full MMO" from a small team: every class, zone, and raid needs ongoing tuning and new content every patch.
- Too many classes at launch; each one multiplies balance work and encounter testing.
- Both a themepark raid ladder and a sandbox full-loot economy; the two fight over where gear comes from.
- Building custom server tech for a single shard before the game loop is proven.
- Authored content cadence the team cannot sustain; a sandbox with player conflict is cheaper per hour of play.
- Launching on many platforms at once with cross-play and shared economy.
- Housing, crafting, PvP, and raids all at launch instead of one core pillar done well.
