# Role-playing games

## Core loop

Explore, take on a goal (quest), overcome challenges (combat, dialogue, puzzles), collect rewards (experience, loot, story), grow the character or party (levels, skills, gear), make choices that shape the story, repeat with harder content.

## Design pillars

- **Character growth:** the player sees their character get stronger or more capable in ways they chose.
- **Meaningful choice:** builds, dialogue and quest decisions have consequences the player can see later.
- **World and story:** a setting worth exploring, with quests that reveal it.
- **Party or build identity:** each companion or build has a distinct role and feel.
- **Readable systems:** players can tell why they won or lost, and what a stat does.

## Common failures

- Stats or skills with no visible effect, so players dump them.
- Choices with no later consequence.
- Global level scaling that removes the feeling of growth.
- Loot power outpacing enemy power, collapsing late-game challenge.
- Fetch quests padding play time.
- Skill trees of small percentage boosts with no new actions.
- Hidden randomness that feels unfair.
- Companions with no mechanical role or personal story.

## Scope traps

- Branching quests multiply writing, voice and testing cost; reconverge with branch and bottleneck.
- Each companion with full reactivity is a large writing load; fewer, deeper companions are cheaper.
- Multiple endings require tracking many flags; test every combination that matters.
- Fully voiced dialogue raises cost for every line and every rewrite.
- Open worlds need content density; empty space reads as padding.
- Many classes each need balance and unique content; start with fewer.
