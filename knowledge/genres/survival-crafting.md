# Survival-crafting

## Core loop

Gather resources, craft tools and shelter, survive a recurring threat (night, weather, hunger, enemies), then use better tools to reach new areas and repeat at a higher tier.

- Short loop (minutes): gather, craft one item, return to safety before the threat arrives.
- Medium loop (hours): reach a new biome or depth, defeat a gate, unlock a new material tier.
- Long loop (whole game): escape, defeat a final boss, or build something worth keeping.

## Design pillars

1. **A clear first deadline.** The first night or first storm teaches the player to build before exploring.
2. **Needs that compete.** Hunger, warmth, light, sanity, and time pull against each other so every trip is a decision.
3. **Material-gated progression.** New tiers open through objects (ores, boss drops, scanned blueprints), not experience levels.
4. **A world worth leaving the base for.** Biomes, depth, or landmarks pull the player outward; logistics friction (carry weight, portal limits) makes the trip matter.
5. **A base with a job.** Defence, weather protection, comfort buffs, or storage give building a purpose.
6. **Readable danger.** The player should see or hear a threat before it kills them.

## Common failures

- **Needs as chores.** A hunger bar that only drains and refills adds busywork without decisions.
- **No first deadline.** Without an early threat, the first hour drifts and players stop before the loop clicks.
- **Recipe sprawl.** Hundreds of recipes with no reason to build most of them. Every recipe should open a verb, a place, or a defence.
- **Wiki dependence.** Recipes that can only be learned outside the game. Plan in-game discovery from the start.
- **Flat late game.** Once survival is solved, nothing changes. Add a world shift, endgame threat, or reset plan.
- **Unreadable death.** Players die to a stat they did not notice or an enemy they could not see.
- **Durability as punishment.** Tools that break mid-task with no warning push players to quit instead of plan.
- **Storage overload.** Unlimited chests make hoarding free and remove inventory decisions.

## Scope traps

- **Recipe count.** Each recipe needs art, balance, UI, and a reason to exist. Start with a small set that each serves the loop.
- **Procedural worlds.** Generation needs tooling, seed testing, and guarantees that key resources spawn. Hand-made maps are often cheaper for a first build.
- **Multiplayer and persistence.** Networking, save sync, griefing, and server hosting can each be as large as the single-player game. Ship single-player first unless co-op is the core.
- **Freeform building.** Snapping, structural rules, and physics for building pieces take a long time to make feel good.
- **Simulated ecosystems.** Animal populations, plant growth, and weather systems interact in ways that are hard to test. Fake them until a playtest shows they matter.
- **Early access drift.** Long early access periods add features faster than the core loop is tightened.
