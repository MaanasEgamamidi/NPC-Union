# Battle royale

## Core loop

Many players enter one match, usually starting with no gear. They drop onto a large map, gather weapons and items, and fight. A safe zone shrinks over time and damages anyone outside it, forcing players together. Eliminated players are out (or get a limited second chance). The last player or team standing wins. Then queue again.

## Design pillars

- Equal start: everyone begins with little, so early choices (where to land) matter.
- Rising pressure: the zone and the shrinking player count raise tension over the match.
- One life with stakes: elimination matters, softened only by deliberate second-chance rules.
- Readable chaos: players must spot threats, hear fights, and plan rotations.
- Short re-entry: after a loss, getting into the next match fast.

## Common failures

- Long stretches with no contact in the early and mid game.
- Early deaths followed by long waits, with no redeploy or fast requeue.
- Final circles that land on open ground or one dominant spot, deciding matches by luck.
- Loot that leaves solo landers without a usable kit, or pickups no one uses.
- Too few concurrent players to fill lobbies, leading to heavy bot backfill.
- A signature mechanic with a skill ceiling so high it pushes new players out.

## Scope traps

- Building a huge map before proving the first two minutes are fun on a small one.
- Promising high lobby counts without budgeting servers, netcode, and anti-cheat.
- Adding vehicles, heroes, and building all at once instead of one signature system.
- Launching without a plan to fill lobbies (population, regions, cross-play).
- Seasonal map and content updates treated as optional when the genre expects them.
