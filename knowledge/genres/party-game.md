# Party games

Games built for a group in one room or one call, where the shared experience matters as much as who wins.

## Core loop

1. Join: players enter with as little friction as possible (pick up a controller, scan a room code, open a lobby).
2. Learn: a one-screen instruction or a single verb teaches the round.
3. Play a round: short, readable, often chaotic play that produces funny moments.
4. Results: scores, reveals or eliminations that give the room something to react to.
5. Play again: the round is short enough that "one more" is an easy yes.

## Design pillars

- Fast to join: no long setup, few controllers needed, or phones as controllers.
- Rules on one screen: anyone can play after a few seconds of instruction.
- Fair across skill levels: luck, comeback systems and varied round types let newcomers win sometimes.
- Fun to watch: spectators, waiting players and eliminated players still enjoy themselves.
- Readable from the couch: clear player colors, big text, strong silhouettes.
- Social moments: the game creates talk, teasing, cooperation or betrayal.

## Common failures

- Winner-take-all scoring that lets the strongest player win every session.
- Luck that decides so much that skilled players feel their choices do not matter.
- Long tutorials or rules that must be memorized before play.
- Downtime: eliminated or waiting players with nothing to watch or do.
- Too few input options, so a group without enough controllers cannot play.
- Poor readability: similar player colors, small text, cluttered screens.
- Online play with no kick, mute, private room or content filter options.
- Rounds that run long after the result is already clear.

## Scope traps

- Minigame count: each minigame needs its own rules, art, balance and testing. A smaller set of polished games beats a large set of rough ones.
- Online multiplayer added late: netcode, matchmaking and moderation are large projects on their own.
- Supporting every input type (controllers, phones, keyboard) at once.
- Many board maps or modes before the core round is fun.
- Large-lobby elimination formats that need many concurrent players to feel alive.
- User-generated content such as custom prompts without a moderation plan.
