# Horror

## Core loop

Explore a hostile space with limited light, information, and resources. Notice signs of a threat (sound, glimpses, environmental clues). Evade, hide, or fight with scarce tools. Reach safety, recover, and save. Repeat with the threat changing or escalating.

## Design pillars

- Vulnerability: the player is weaker than the threat, or has too few resources to be safe.
- Anticipation over contact: dread built through sound and partial sight.
- Rhythm of tension and release: safe rooms and quiet stretches make spikes land.
- Uncertainty: limited vision, unreliable maps, threats whose rules are not fully known.
- Meaning: psychological horror ties the scares to a theme or a character.

## Common failures

- Showing the monster in full too early or too often, so it becomes familiar.
- Constant tension with no release, which leads to numbness.
- Jump scares used as filler instead of payoff.
- Giving the player enough power that the threat becomes an obstacle to clear.
- Death costing too much time, which turns fear into frustration.
- A stalker AI that feels like it cheats, or one so predictable it can be gamed.
- Darkness so heavy the player cannot read the space and stops exploring.

## Scope traps

- Smart stalker AI takes long tuning; budget playtest time for it, not just build time.
- Audio is half the scare; a late or thin sound pass undercuts everything.
- Many creature types each need animation, audio, and behavior; one strong threat with changing rules is often cheaper and scarier.
- Hand-placed scripted scares do not scale to procedural or long games.
- Lighting and fog tuning per room is labor-heavy; plan tools early.
