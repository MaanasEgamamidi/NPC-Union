# NASA International Space Apps Challenge

An annual two-day global hackathon where teams use open data from NASA and partner space agencies to answer challenges written by NASA subject matter experts. Free, open to all ages and skill levels, in person at local events or online via the Universal Event.

Access note: www.spaceappschallenge.org serves an incomplete TLS chain (leaf issued by "SSL.com TLS Issuing RSA CA R1", sent with a cross-sign to a root many stores no longer carry). Fetching the intermediate from the leaf's AIA URL (http://cert.ssl.com/SSL.com-TLS-I-RSA-R1.cer, issued by "SSL.com TLS RSA Root CA 2022") completes the chain with full verification. ## Structure and rules (2022 guides, the latest full text checked)

Sources: 2022 Project Submission Guide and 2022 Judging & Awards Guide (PDF links in frontmatter). Re-check the current year's guides; these are a baseline.

- Two-day hackathon. Submission opened 9:00 AM local on day one and closed 11:59 PM local on day two, by the registered location's time zone; Universal Event teams use the team creator's local time.
- Team size 1 to 6. No members added after submission closes.
- Required fields: Project Title, High-Level Project Summary, Link to Final Project, Link to Project Demo, Detailed Project Description, Space Agency Data, Hackathon Journey, References.
- Demo: a 30-second video or a 7-slide deck, one link; judges stop at the limit. The Demo is distinct from the Final Project ("if your team creates a website, a game, or an algorithm, THAT is your Final Project").
- Links must be public with no login or permission request. The page accepts images, not videos or documents.
- Global Judging eligibility: answer an official challenge ("Invent Your Own Challenge" not eligible), all members listed, all required fields, English (non-English video needs English subtitles), no profanity, on-time submission, agree to Terms, list all external resources in References, use at least some open data from NASA or a Space Agency Partner.
- The guide sets expectations: teams are not expected to have a finished product, only progress toward a solution.

## Judging

Three phases (2022 guide):
1. Local Event judges pick 1-3 projects per location as Global Nominees; the Universal Event (about 40 subject matter experts in 2022) advances roughly one in ten. Local events may give their own local prizes.
2. Global Judging by NASA and partner-agency experts selects Global Finalists.
3. Executive Judging by agency leaders selects one winner per Global Award.

Criteria (2022): **Impact** (size of problem, how many helped), **Creativity** (novel vs incremental), **Validity** (scientifically valid, works in the real world), **Relevance** (responsive to the challenge, complete, feasible, usable), **Presentation** (the story: challenge, solution, why it matters). The guide stresses equal attention to Presentation. Current-year wording: UNVERIFIED (the 2026 Judging & Awards guide page did not return readable text).

## Global awards

2025 names (homepage and NASA 2025 release): Best Use of Science, Best Use of Data, Best Use of Technology, Galactic Impact, Best Mission Concept, Most Inspirational, Best Use of Storytelling, Global Connection, Art & Technology, Local Impact.

2022 definitions (Judging & Awards Guide): Best Use of Science (best and most valid use of science or the scientific method); Best Use of Data (makes space data accessible, or leverages it to a unique application); Best Use of Technology (most innovative use of technology); Galactic Impact (most potential to improve life on Earth or in the universe); Best Mission Concept (most plausible concept and design); Most Inspirational ("captures our hearts"); Best Storytelling (communicates the potential of open data through storytelling); Global Connection (connects people around the world through technology); Art & Technology (combines technical and creative skills); Local Impact (greatest potential for local impact).

Names vary by year: 2020 used Data, Mission, Impact, Science, Technology, Inspirational (with a tie) plus a Euro Data Cube Award; 2019 had Best Use of Hardware and six awards in total. The 2022 guide described a Winners Trip to a NASA Center (travel self-funded by guests); check the current guide for prizes.

## Timeline of a typical year

1. Registration opens months ahead (2025: July, per NASA's 2024 release).
2. Challenges published before the weekend; each has Summary, Objectives, Resources tab.
3. Hackathon weekend (October 2018-2025; November 14-15 in 2026).
4. Global Nominees, then Global Finalists posted on the site.
5. Global Winners announced in a livestream: Jan 23, 2024 (2023 event), Jan 16, 2025 (2024 event), Dec 18, 2025 (2025 event).
